## Best Loopio alternatives for federal proposals

If you run federal proposals and want Loopio alternatives, here are the short lists that actually work. For small and mid-size teams, look at Privia, Expedience Software, Zbizlink, SiftHub, and GovHub. For larger enterprises, add Responsive, Upland Qvidian, XaitPorter, and RocketDocs. Each covers a different slice of the work, from answer libraries to Word-based authoring to Section L and M compliance.

Quick take. If your main pain is answering recurring RFP questions, Loopio, SiftHub, and Responsive are closest. If your pain is building a compliance matrix from Section L, then authoring a compliant Volume 1 in Word, look at Privia, Expedience, or a tool that can do true RFP shredding and matrix export. If you need color team workflows, document control, and on-prem or private cloud, shortlist Privia, XaitPorter, and RocketDocs.

## Loopio pricing and Loopio cost, what we see in 2026

Loopio does not post public pricing. From quotes our readers and buyers have shared with us in the last 18 months, expect the following ranges. These are for guidance only and depend on seats, onboarding, and add-ons.

- Core package with a small team, 5 to 10 users. 18,000 to 35,000 dollars per year.
- Mid-market with 15 to 30 users, advanced content governance and SSO. 40,000 to 75,000 dollars per year.
- Enterprise, 50 plus users, multi-workspace, security addenda, and premium support. 80,000 to 150,000 dollars per year.

Watch the fine print. Many vendors charge for implementation, content migration from SharePoint or Google Drive, and premium integrations. Expect 3,000 to 20,000 dollars one-time for setup if you want help with answer library builds and workflow templates.

## Loopio competitors to consider, federal use cases only

Here are the common Loopio competitors, with the federal angle called out.

- SiftHub. AI-first knowledge base that can auto-answer RFP questionnaires and security forms. Strong at Q and A. Still maturing for strict Section L to M compliance packaging and page count control.
- Responsive, formerly RFPIO competitor in the same category as Loopio. Scales at enterprise. Good SSO, audit trails, and content governance. Less federal-specific support for Section L and M workflows out of the box. We wrote up related tradeoffs in our post on [RFPIO alternatives for federal contractors](/blog/rfpio-alternative-federal-contractors/).
- Privia. Longtime federal staple for proposal collaboration. Strong document control, versioning, and review workflows. Plays well with Word-based authoring, color team cycles, and hard page limits.
- Expedience Software. Microsoft Word add-in for templates, styles, boilerplate, and compliance outlines. Favored by teams that live in Word and must hit exact formats, fonts, and page limits.
- RocketDocs. Content library and proposal automation, more enterprise leaning. Often used by regulated industries. Useful when you need mature content controls and ISO style governance.
- XaitPorter. Structured document authoring and workflow. Heavyweight, supports very large teams and complex document assembly. Consider it if you need strict process controls.
- Zbizlink. SMB friendly proposal management. Lower cost entry, covers pipeline, teaming, templates, and content library. Useful for firms building their first formal process.
- Qvidian by Upland. Strong content automation for large orgs. Less federal-specific guidance, strong in commercial.
- Microsoft 365 plus add-ins. SharePoint, Teams, and Word with macros and styles can cover 70 percent of the job if you have process maturity. You give up automated shredding and structured libraries unless you add point tools.

Hard truth. Many RFP tools are built for commercial security questionnaires and marketing RFIs. Federal work needs Section L and Section M alignment, page count control, strict file naming, font and margin compliance, and handling of forms like SF 33, SF 1449, and pricing spreadsheets. If a vendor cannot show a real compliance matrix and a clean crosswalk to a Word outline in the demo, they will cost you time in Red Team.

## Loopio vs SiftHub, what to know before you pick

- Content source of truth. Both use a central answer library. SiftHub leans harder on AI to mine past proposals and Slack or Teams. Loopio is more structured with manual curation and tags.
- Federal compliance features. Neither ships a true Section L to Section M matrix builder by default. If you need automated shredding of L and C into a matrix with compliance statements, you will need a supplement or a different tool.
- Security claims. Ask both about SOC 2 Type II, data residency, and how they handle CUI. If you are subject to DFARS 252.204-7012 and NIST SP 800-171, ask about FedRAMP Moderate or a private cloud option. Most commercial RFP tools do not hold FedRAMP authorizations. Get it in writing if you must store CUI.
- Workflow. Loopio has mature assignments, due dates, and reviews. SiftHub is newer but moves fast on AI assisted writing. Test how each exports to Word with your styles, not their sample templates.

Decision tip. Bring a real federal RFP excerpt to the demo. Use Section L, Section M, and one technical factor. Make them create a compliance crosswalk, draft a two page section in your template, and export without breaking your table of contents. If they need three workarounds, keep looking. For more on why this matters, see our write up on [Section L and M driven work](/solutions/rfp-shredding/).

## Loopio alternatives for enterprise teams

If you have 100 plus users, multiple business units, or InfoSec policies that block multi-tenant SaaS, start here.

- Responsive. Enterprise IAM integrations, content governance, multi-workspace. Solid support model.
- XaitPorter. Structured authoring and strong auditability. Good when you need strict change control and complex approvals.
- RocketDocs. Mature library governance and regulated industry experience.
- Qvidian. Large content operations, deep search, and business rules.

What to check. SSO and MFA, SCIM user provisioning, private cloud or single tenant options, data retention, and content lifecycle controls. Ask for documented SOC 2 Type II, penetration testing cadence, and disaster recovery RTO and RPO. If your teams handle CUI, ask about encryption for data at rest and in transit, customer managed keys, and data segregation.

## Buyer checklist, choose a Loopio alternative that fits FAR work

Security and compliance

- If you touch CUI or CDI for DoD, require NIST SP 800-171 controls and DFARS 252.204-7012 flowdown support. Ask how they handle incident reporting in 72 hours.
- For civilian work with sensitive PII, ask for SOC 2 Type II. If your agency or prime requires FedRAMP Moderate, confirm authorization or a documented path. Many vendors will not qualify.
- Require encryption at rest and in transit, SSO with SAML 2.0, MFA, audit logs, and data export on termination.

RFP shredding and compliance

- Can the tool read Section L and Section C, then produce a line by line compliance matrix with IDs, page limits, and responsible owners. This must map back to Section M. FAR 15.304 makes evaluation factors controlling. Your matrix must follow that structure.
- Does it support hard page limits, fonts, and margins. Many solicitations will be rejected for simple format misses.
- Can you export to Word with your styles, cross references, tables, and figure numbering intact.

Content and authorship

- Answer library with sources, last used date, owner, and approvals. Stale boilerplate will hurt Past Performance and Management sections.
- AI assist that respects your policy. Require approval steps and a clear log of machine generated content.
- Support for common forms and attachments. SF 33 or SF 1449 cover pages, J and L attachments, and pricing spreadsheets that must be kept in sync.

Process and reviews

- Assignments by volume, section, and requirement ID. Due dates with reminders.
- Color team workflows that fit your gates. Pink, Red, and Gold need clean inputs and tracked actions.
- Clean compare and merge for editor heavy work. Track changes across writers without corrupting the master.

Commercial terms

- Clear seat definitions, named vs concurrent. Admin and reviewer roles included.
- Implementation scope, data migration, and template building, priced and scheduled. Make them commit to outputs you can use in week one.
- Data ownership, customer exit plan, and 30 day data export in a usable format.

## Worked example, scoring a tool on a real RFP

Scenario. You are a 35 person SDVOSB bidding a best value tradeoff per FAR 15.101 on a DHS IT support RFP. The RFP has Section L instructions for a 25 page Technical Volume, a 10 page Management Volume, Past Performance questionnaires, and a Pricing Volume on the supplied spreadsheet. Fonts must be 12 point Times New Roman with 1 inch margins. File naming and bookmarks are specified in Section L. Evaluation factors in Section M are Technical Approach, Management, Past Performance, and Price.

What you bring to the demo

- Section L and Section M PDFs. One sample task order. Your Word template with styles and a two level TOC.
- Three common Q and A items from past RFPs. One CUI scenario to test security claims.

What to make the vendor do in 45 minutes

- Shred Section L into a compliance matrix. Must include requirement ID, volume, page limit, and evaluation factor mapping to Section M. No manual copy and paste help from their team.
- Build a Word outline from that matrix. Export to your template, with correct heading styles and placeholder tables and figures. No broken cross references after export.
- Populate one common answer from their library or AI into the Management section. Show source, owner, and last used date. Demonstrate an approval step.
- Route the Technical Approach draft to a Red Team reviewer. Show how comments and changes will be merged back to the master without breaking numbering.
- Create the final submission set. Correct file names, bookmarks as required, and a compliance check that flags any over limit pages or missing attachments.

Scoring rubric, 1 to 5 for each line. Total out of 25.

- Matrix quality, accuracy and speed.
- Word export fidelity and template control.
- Content governance and approvals.
- Review workflow and merge stability.
- Submission packaging and checks.

If a tool scores under 18, it will cost you hours each cycle. If they cannot create a usable matrix and a clean export, they are not built for federal work. You can write your own rubric, but hold this line. Too many buyers accept a slideware demo and pay for it in Pink and Red Team.
