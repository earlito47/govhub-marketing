## Loopio competitors, ranked for federal proposals

If you answer Section L and Section M driven RFPs, start with tools that handle compliance matrices, versioned volumes, and secure content libraries. Top Loopio competitors we see performing well on federal work: RFPIO, RFP360, Upland Qvidian, Expedience Software, XaitPorter, RocketDocs, Privia, and homegrown SharePoint plus Word or Excel with macros. Each has tradeoffs for FAR Part 15 proposals and SF forms.

Quick snapshot from recent SMB quotes we have seen in 2025 and 2026, for about 10 named users. RFPIO, 18k to 45k per year, optional add ons. RFP360, 12k to 30k per year. Loopio for reference, 15k to 40k per year. Qvidian, 25k to 60k per year. Expedience, 9k to 20k per year plus Microsoft 365. XaitPorter, 30k to 70k per year. RocketDocs, 20k to 45k per year. Privia, 15k to 35k per year. Implementation fees from 2k to 10k are common.

If your pipeline is mostly questionnaires and commercial RFPs, Loopio and RFPIO shine. If your pipeline is federal proposals under FAR Part 15 with strict Section L and M structure, evaluate tools that build and export compliance matrices, support color team reviews, and do not mangle your Word styles.

## Loopio alternatives for federal proposals

Here is how the main competitors line up for federal work.

- RFPIO. Strong answer library, decent collaboration, AI assisted answers. Solid on questionnaires. Usable for federal if you structure by volumes and link to requirements. Watch Word export fidelity and how it handles custom styles in long technical volumes.

- RFP360. Similar profile to RFPIO. Clean interface for intake and questionnaires. Federal teams will need to validate Section L and M mapping and compliance matrix export.

- Upland Qvidian. Old but capable enterprise tool. Strong content library and proposal assembly. Heavier admin. Word export is reliable, which matters when an RFP dictates fonts, margins, and page limits per FAR 15.204 Part IV.

- Expedience Software. Built inside Word and Excel. This fits teams that want maximum control of styles, cover pages, and volumes. Great when the RFP prescribes exact formatting, such as SF 330 submissions or page limits with figures counting at 50 percent. You trade AI features for precision and simplicity.

- XaitPorter. Structured authoring and workflow. Good for large, multi volume efforts. Check whether your reviewers are comfortable leaving Word to comment and whether you can export to Word without reformatting.

- RocketDocs. Answer library focused. Suitable if you field many questionnaires and DD254 or vendor forms. Validate support for compliance matrices and Section M traceability.

- Privia. Proposal centric collaboration with flexible workflows, document check in and check out, and review cycles. Strong for color team reviews. Make sure you can build a machine readable compliance matrix that traces to every paragraph of Section L and M.

- SharePoint plus Word and Excel. The baseline every shop knows. You can deliver with this if you run tight templates, disciplined checklists, and macros to extract requirement IDs. No licenses beyond Microsoft 365. The hard parts are content hygiene and keeping version control straight when you have multiple volumes.

None of the above replace reading the RFP. To refresh how Section L and M drive your outline and compliance, see [How to Read a Federal RFP Section L and M](/blog/read-federal-rfp-section-l-m/).

## Must have features for FAR Part 15 and Section L and M work

- Requirements capture that respects the Uniform Contract Format. FAR 15.204 Part IV puts Instructions to Offerors in Section L and Evaluation Factors in Section M. Your tool must tag each requirement to L and trace to the matching factor in M. It should export a CSV or Excel matrix for internal reviews and for protest defense if needed.

- Compliance matrix builder and export. The matrix should include requirement ID, verbatim text, source page and paragraph, proposal location, owner, due date, status, and verification against M factors. If you are new to this, start with [What Is a Compliance Matrix](/blog/what-is-a-compliance-matrix/).

- Word faithful exports. Federal RFPs often set fonts, margins, line spacing, and page limits. A tool that changes your styles or introduces hidden formatting will burn time during Pink and Red Teams. Ask vendors to export a 30 page technical volume using your styles and show the Word file, not a PDF.

- Color team workflow that matches how you work. Pink, Red, and Gold Teams are not optional on larger bids. You need page level commenting, change tracking, and role based assignments. If your reviewers refuse to leave Word, make sure the tool supports Word based reviews. If they can work in a browser, require granular section locks.

- Security for CUI. If you store Controlled Unclassified Information, DFARS 252.204-7012 requires you to safeguard it and to meet NIST SP 800-171 controls. You likely do not need FedRAMP for a vendor tool used by your company, but you do need SSO, audit logs, data residency clarity, and a path to a signed BAA if you ever store ITAR or HIPAA content. Ask for a current SOC 2 Type II report.

- Forms and attachments support. You will encounter SF 33, SF 1449, SF 1442, and numerous agency templates. The tool should manage attachments with correct file names and allow easy inclusion in volumes or appendices. For A E work, check SF 330 support and resume templating.

- Past performance and CPARS capture. The system should store contract numbers, dollar values, period of performance, and CPARS quotes for quick reuse that still respects RFP limits. FAR 15.304 lists Past Performance as a typical evaluation factor. Your library needs fields that map to that factor.

- AI that respects the source. AI can draft a first pass, but it must cite the requirement ID and link back to source text. Blind AI that hallucinates will hurt you at Red Team and may violate 52.212-1 if you miss an instruction.

## Worked example, shredding L and M into a compliance matrix

Scenario. DoD issues an RFP under FAR Part 15 with the Uniform Contract Format. Section L has instructions for Volume I, Executive, 10 pages, Volume II, Technical, 30 pages, Volume III, Past Performance, 10 pages, and Volume IV, Price, no page limit. Section M lists factors, Technical, Past Performance, Price. Best value tradeoff per FAR 15.304 and 15.101-1.

Goal. Build a compliance matrix that assigns every L instruction to a proposal location and ties each instruction to the relevant M factor for evaluation.

Steps.

1. Extract L paragraphs. Use OCR only if needed, then normalize headings. Assign IDs like L.3.2.a. Capture page number and paragraph.

2. For each L item, decide owner and deliverable. Example, Volume II, Technical, L.3.2.a says, Describe your approach to software maintenance and patching within 72 hours of release. Assign to Cyber Lead.

3. Map to M. Find the matching M paragraph. M.2.a says, The Government will evaluate the Offeror's approach to cybersecurity operations for responsiveness and risk. Link L.3.2.a to M.2.a.

4. Plan proof. Add a column, Evidence. Here, include our internal SOP, a sample patch plan, and a recent CPARS quote if allowed. If the RFP invokes 52.212-2 for evaluation of commercial items, confirm the factor mapping fits the language there.

5. Set proposal location. Volume II, Section 2.1, 2 pages, includes a table with patch timelines and roles. Reference the outline heading and the Word style you will use.

6. Review status. Track Pink Team complete, Red Team edits pending, Gold Team signoff done.

Your matrix row might look like this in Excel.

- Requirement ID. L.3.2.a
- Requirement text. Describe your approach to software maintenance and patching within 72 hours of release.
- Source. Section L, page 47, para 3
- Evaluation factor. M.2.a Cybersecurity operations approach
- Proposal location. Vol II, 2.1 Approach to Patching, pages 6 to 7
- Owner. Cyber Lead
- Evidence. SOP-SEC-004, Patch Plan v3, CPARS excerpt 2024
- Status. Red Team in progress

## Checklist, pick a Loopio alternative in one day

Use this during vendor calls and trials.

- Can I import a real RFP PDF, identify L and M, and export an editable compliance matrix with IDs and page numbers?
- Will the tool export my 30 page Technical Volume to Word using my styles without reflowing figures or tables?
- Can reviewers comment inline during Pink and Red Teams without creating parallel copies?
- Does the answer library support fields we need for Past Performance, including contract number, POP, dollar value, and CPARS quote length limits?
- Can I restrict library access by contract or customer to avoid cross use where it is not allowed?
- Does the vendor have SOC 2 Type II and support SSO and MFA? Will they sign DFARS 252.204-7012 flow down language on request?
- Can I build a template that matches the RFP volumes, not just a single binder document?
- Does AI cite requirement IDs and point back to source text in L and M so my editors can verify?
- Can I bulk rename and package attachments, including SF forms and appendices, to match the RFP file naming scheme?
- What is the true annual cost for my seat count plus the integrations I need, and what is the one time implementation fee?

Time box your trials. Hand the vendor a sanitized RFP and 10 pages of prior content. Ask them to show the entire workflow, import to export, live.

## When Loopio is still the right choice

If your team fields hundreds of vendor questionnaires, due diligence forms, or short commercial RFPs, Loopio remains strong. The answer library and automation can pay for itself quickly in that use case. For classic FAR Part 15 multi volume proposals, Loopio can still work if you bolt on a compliance matrix process and keep authors in Word for final formatting, but you will manage more by hand.

## Bottom line

Pick the tool that matches your pipeline. If you submit to federal buyers that grade you against Section M and throw out non compliant bids under 52.212-1 or FAR 15.208, you need precise mapping, clean Word exports, and strong review flow.
