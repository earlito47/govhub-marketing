## GovWin IQ pricing, what users actually pay

For small and mid-size contractors, real quotes for GovWin IQ usually land in these ranges. Single federal seat, 4,500 to 7,500 dollars per year. Federal plus state and local, 7,000 to 12,000 dollars per year. Add a second or third seat, often 1,500 to 3,000 dollars per seat. Task order data for major IDIQs can add 1,500 to 4,000 dollars. Multi-year terms discount 10 to 20 percent. Month to month is not common. Most contracts are 12 months, billed annually, with auto renewal. Negotiated notices to cancel are typically 30 to 60 days before the term ends, check your order form.

If your quote is outside those bands, it is likely a different module mix, a promotional rate, or you are being quoted enterprise support tiers. Ask for a line item breakdown by module and by seat.

## Deltek GovWin pricing tiers and modules explained

Deltek sells GovWin IQ by market scope and features. Packages get renamed often, but the building blocks tend to be the same.

Common modules you will see on quotes:

- Federal opportunities and intelligence. Core feed of pre-RFP, RFI, sources sought, and solicitation data for federal buyers. Includes analyst notes on many large programs.
- State and local opportunities. SLED content, which includes cities, counties, school districts, and higher ed. This replaced the legacy Onvia product years ago.
- Task orders and vehicles. Deeper coverage for IDIQ, GWAC, and BPA task orders. Helpful if you sit under vehicles like GSA MAS, OASIS Plus, CIO-SP4, or agency IDIQs.
- Company and contact data. Agency org charts, decision maker contact info, incumbents, and teaming partners.
- Smart matching and alerts. Saved searches, Smart Fit or similar scoring, and email alerts by NAICS, PSC, keyword, and agency.

What changes price:

- Scope. Federal only is cheaper. Federal plus SLED costs more. Adding task orders is additive.
- Seats. The first seat is the most expensive. Additional seats are discounted. Minimums vary by package.
- Data rights. Some quotes limit bulk exports or API use. Lifts on limits can add cost.
- Term. Two and three year terms usually get 10 to 20 percent off. New fiscal year and quarter ends are the best times to ask.

## GovWin vs GovWin IQ vs Deltek GovWin

Buyers search these terms interchangeably. Deltek is the vendor. GovWin is the brand. GovWin IQ is the subscription product line that includes the modules above. If the quote says Deltek GovWin, it is the same family. Focus on what is in scope and how many seats, not the label.

## Is GovWin worth it for small firms

It depends on how disciplined your pipeline is. If you chase anything with a match on your NAICS code, you will burn cash. If you use it to shape deals early and kill bad fits fast, it pays for itself.

Use it for three jobs that matter:

- Pre-RFP shaping. Follow recompetes 12 to 24 months out, track sources sought and RFIs, and start teaming. That moves your Pwin more than any tool you buy.
- Task order speed. If you live on IDIQs and BPAs, you need fast alerts and historical trends to pick the right task orders.
- Bid or no-bid discipline. Use intel to kill low Pwin work quickly so you can invest in two or three real shots.

## Hidden costs, contract terms, and renewals

- Auto renewal. Expect auto renewal language with a notice window, often 30 to 60 days before term end. Calendar the date when you sign.
- Seats that do not float. Most licenses are named users. If you share logins, you violate terms and hurt adoption.
- Add-on creep. Mid-year add-ons usually co-terminate and renew at list minus your discount. Ask for the future renewal number in writing.
- Training time. Plan two to four hours to set up searches, alerts, and dashboards. If you skip this, your alerts will be noise.
- Data exports. If you need CSV exports to feed CRM, confirm daily or monthly caps. Exports can be limited by module.

## How many seats do you really need

- One seat. Owner-operator, or a capture manager who both sources and qualifies. Add a second seat when proposal volume forces context switching.
- Two to three seats. Typical for firms with one capture lead, one BD researcher, and one proposal manager who needs direct intel access.
- Four plus seats. Only if you run multiple capture lanes or have dedicated SLED and federal teams.

You can get by with one seat and a weekly pipeline review if your team is small. Pull the pipeline, make decisions, assign actions, then shut GovWin and write. ## Negotiating your GovWin IQ price

- Ask for line items. Get Federal, SLED, task orders, and seat counts on separate rows. You cannot negotiate what you cannot see.
- Use timing. End of quarter and fiscal year end get better discounts. Federal Q4 is strong.
- Multi-year with protection. Offer a two year term for 15 to 20 percent off. Ask for a cap on year two increases for any added seats.
- Bundle smart, not wide. Do not add SLED unless you have a plan to pursue it. Do not buy task orders unless you sit under vehicles.
- Reference peers. Say you expect the single seat to land between 5k and 7k, and an add-on seat between 1.5k and 3k, given similar shops.
- Renewal proof. Ask for a pro forma renewal quote in the order file, same discounts, same scope, subject to CPI cap.

## What about free and lower cost options

You can build a decent pipeline without GovWin if your targets are simple.

Free sources

- SAM.gov Contract Opportunities. Solicitations, RFIs, sources sought. Good alerts. Weak on pre-RFP intel.
- Agency forecasts. Program roadmaps live on each agency site. Quality varies.
- USAspending.gov and FPDS Atom data. Past awards and trends. Good for recompete timing.

Lower cost paid tools

- Tools that scrape and aggregate SAM and state portals. Prices are often 1k to 3k per year. Coverage varies.
- Niche tools for IDIQ task orders. If you live under one vehicle, ask the PMO what they provide first.

## Worked ROI example

Assumptions

- Average annual GovWin IQ cost for a two seat federal package with task orders, 9,500 dollars after discount.
- Your average proposal fully burdened cost, 18,000 dollars. That includes staff hours, color team time, and any outside help.
- You submit 12 proposals per year now, win 2. Your Pwin is 16.7 percent.

Use GovWin IQ for shaping and faster kills

- You track recompetes 18 months out and pre-position with two primes. You join one team early and prime one.
- You kill 4 of 12 bids early due to incumbency, budget, or LPTA under FAR 15.101-2 combined with a price you cannot meet.
- You reallocate effort to 8 bids and improve Pwin to 25 percent through early shaping and tighter compliance.

Math

- Proposals avoided, 4 x 18,000 equals 72,000 saved.
- Pwin lift, 8 bids x 25 percent equals 2 wins. You match prior year wins with fewer bids, or you keep 12 bids and target 3 wins.
- Tool cost, 9,500 dollars. Net savings on proposal spend alone, 62,500 dollars. That ignores revenue and CPARS effects.

## When GovWin is not worth it

- Your pipeline is mostly micro-purchases under FAR 13.2 at 10,000 dollars or less. Those do not hit paid intel platforms.
- You only chase task orders from one IDIQ and the PMO already emails a clean feed of orders.
- You lack proposal capacity. If you cannot staff the writing, buying more intel will not fix throughput.

## FAQs

What is the difference between GovWin and GovWin IQ

- GovWin is the brand. GovWin IQ is the subscription product line. Quotes may say Deltek GovWin or GovWin IQ, your job is to confirm modules and seats.

Are there seat minimums

- Some packages have a two seat minimum. Others allow a single seat. Ask before you assume.

Can I start with federal and add SLED later

- Yes. Most buyers start with federal only, then add SLED if the model proves out.

What about data exports and CRM integration

- Most packages allow CSV exports. API or bulk export rights can be limited or priced. Confirm caps if you plan to sync daily to CRM.

How accurate are the prices in this post

- These are the ranges we see on small firm quotes in 2023 and 2024. Your quote can be higher or lower based on scope, term, and timing. Always get a written breakdown.

## Quick checklist for buying GovWin IQ

- Define scope. Federal, SLED, task orders. Write this on one page with your top 20 target agencies and vehicles.
- Decide seat count. Name the users and what each will own, research, capture, or proposal.
- Build searches. NAICS, PSC, agencies, and 10 to 20 strong keywords. Kill generic terms that flood alerts.
- Set alert cadence. Daily for task orders, daily or weekly for federal pre-RFP, weekly for SLED.
- Calendar the renewal. Put the notice date on three calendars. Add a 90 day review task.
- Track ROI. Wins, proposal kills, time saved. Review in a monthly pipeline meeting.
