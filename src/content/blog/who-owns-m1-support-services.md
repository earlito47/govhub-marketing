## Who owns M1 Support Services?

Short answer. M1 Support Services, L.P. is a privately held Texas limited partnership. There is no publicly traded parent on record. Federal procurement systems do not publish individual beneficial owners. The authoritative public sources you can check are the Immediate Owner and Highest Level Owner fields in SAM.gov, which are required by FAR 52.204-17, and the state business filings in Texas that list the general partner of the limited partnership.

If you need a name, you must pull it yourself from those two sources. SAM.gov will show the legal owner entities on file. The Texas Secretary of State or the Texas Comptroller Taxable Entity Search will show the general partner and registered agent for the partnership. No federal site will give you a list of individual family members or private investors.

## Is M1 Support Services publicly traded or PE-backed?

It is not publicly traded. There is no SEC ticker. In SAM.gov, look at the Highest Level Owner field. If you see an LLC or LP rather than a public company, you are dealing with a private owner of record. If a private equity fund owns the company through a holding entity, that holding entity would appear in the SAM ownership fields.

Two quick system checks confirm this.

- FAR 52.204-17 requires offerors to disclose their Immediate Owner and Highest Level Owner in SAM with UEI or CAGE codes.
- FAR 52.204-10 requires reporting of top executive compensation in some cases. If those names appear in USAspending.gov for M1, it helps you confirm senior leadership, not ownership.

## How to verify M1 ownership in five minutes

You can verify M1’s owner of record without guesswork. Follow this exactly.

1) SAM.gov entity record
- Log in to SAM.gov. Search for the entity name “M1 Support Services, L.P.”
- Open the active registration. Go to Reps and Certs, then Ownership or Control of Offeror. The Immediate Owner and Highest Level Owner fields will show the legal entities that control M1 for procurement purposes, with UEI or CAGE. This mapping is required by FAR 52.204-17 and FAR 52.204-18.

2) CAGE and UEI cross-check
- Note M1’s CAGE and UEI. In SAM, click through to the Immediate Owner and Highest Level Owner entries. Each will have its own CAGE or UEI. This confirms whether M1 is stand-alone or part of a holding stack.

3) Texas state filings
- Use the Texas Comptroller’s Taxable Entity Search to confirm the legal name “M1 Support Services, L.P.”
- Open the listing. For Texas LPs you can identify the general partner entity and registered agent. The general partner is the controlling owner of record for the LP. If you want the formation documents, order them from the Texas Secretary of State.

4) Executive compensation check
- In USAspending.gov, look up the prime recipient “M1 Support Services, L.P.” If FAR 52.204-10 thresholds are met and the data is not otherwise public, the site will list the five most highly compensated executives. That confirms leadership, which can be useful for BD, even though it is not an ownership roster.

5) Record your sources
- Export the SAM ownership fields to PDF. Save a screenshot of the Texas search. Put dates on both. These fields change after M&A or reorganizations. Never rely on a two-year-old article or a cached profile.

## Why ownership matters to you

- Teaming and OCI. Ownership informs organizational conflicts under FAR Subpart 9.5. If the same parent owns a competitor or a support contractor on the same program, that is a red flag.
- Small business go, no-go. Parent ownership can force affiliation under SBA rules at 13 CFR 121. If a holding company’s receipts push the firm over the NAICS size standard, it is not small for that buy.
- Past performance narratives. If M1 is the incumbent and the parent also owns a key sub, your discriminator needs to address continuity and surge, not generic experience.
- BD targeting. If a private owner has a known acquisition pattern in a niche, watch their pipeline. Ownership predicts the next bid almost as well as CPARS.

## Worked example: a 10-minute ownership and control check on M1

You have a draft teaming NDA from M1 on a fast-moving aircraft maintenance RFP. Before you sign, you want owner of record, principal place of business, and any parent ties.

- Minute 1 to 2. Open SAM.gov. Search “M1 Support Services, L.P.” Confirm Active status, expiration date, and that the name matches the NDA. Copy the UEI and CAGE.
- Minute 3. In Reps and Certs, open Ownership or Control of Offeror. Note Immediate Owner and Highest Level Owner. Download the entity overview as PDF. If Immediate Owner is “None,” write that down. If there is an owner, capture that UEI and CAGE as well.
- Minute 4. Check the Points of Contact tab for the contracts administrator name and email. If it does not match your NDA signatory, ask why.
- Minute 5. Open USAspending.gov. Search Prime Awards, Recipient Name equals “M1 Support Services, L.P.” Sort by Action Date, newest first. Note agency, NAICS, IDV links, and place of performance. If Exec Comp data is present, record the names.
- Minute 6 to 7. Open the Texas Comptroller Taxable Entity Search. Enter “M1 Support Services, L.P.” Confirm status is active. Record the general partner entity and registered agent. If the general partner is an LLC with a similar name, that is common in LP structures.
- Minute 8. In FPDS, search by CAGE. Filter to the last 24 months. Export award list. Look for parent company names in the “Contractor Legal Entity Name” and any corporate changes noted in “Vendor Doing Business As Name.”
- Minute 9. Compare the NAICS you see on recent awards with your current RFP. Check the small business size standard on SAM or SBA’s table for that NAICS. Decide if M1 is large or small for this specific bid.
- Minute 10. Write a one-paragraph note for your file. Include date, SAM owner fields, Texas general partner, CAGE, UEI, active status, and a one-line summary of last two awards. Attach your PDFs. Now you can sign the NDA with eyes open.

## Common mistakes when people ask “who owns M1?”

- Treating LinkedIn as authoritative. Job titles drift. Ownership does not. Use SAM and state records.
- Confusing executives with owners. FAR 52.204-10 can list executives. That is not the same as the legal owner of record.
- Relying on an old DD Form 254 or incumbent org chart. Those show roles, not equity.
- Assuming beneficial ownership is public. Since 2024, FinCEN collects beneficial ownership information under 31 CFR 1010.380. That database is not open to the public. Plan on using SAM and state filings.

## Is M1 a good teaming partner on aircraft maintenance bids?

Judge by recent performance and staffing depth, not by hearsay. Pull CPARS if the agency has posted it. Look at surge metrics and fill rates on similar bases. Also map their current active awards to your period of performance to catch over-commit risk.

## Quick reference: rules that control the data you will see

- FAR 52.204-16. Commercial and Government Entity Code Reporting. You will see CAGE codes for the offeror and for owners if reported.
- FAR 52.204-17. Ownership or Control of Offeror. Requires disclosure in SAM of Immediate Owner and Highest Level Owner with CAGE or UEI.
- FAR 52.204-18. Commercial and Government Entity Code Maintenance. Keeps those codes current.
- FAR 52.204-20. Predecessor of Offeror. Flags if the entity is a successor to a prior company, which matters after mergers.
- FAR 52.204-10. Reporting Executive Compensation and First-Tier Subcontract Awards. Sometimes gives you executive names. Not the cap table.
- FAR Subpart 9.5. Organizational conflicts of interest. Ownership can trigger issues here.
- 13 CFR 121. SBA size and affiliation rules. Ownership can change small business status for a specific NAICS.

M1 Support Services is privately held. You will not find a public parent in SEC filings. The only reliable way to answer “who owns M1” is to pull the ownership fields in SAM.gov and the Texas LP general partner from state records, then save your work with dates. Anything else is rumor.
