## Who owns M1 Support Services?

M1 Support Services, L.P. is privately held. As of late 2024, federal vendor records do not list a highest-level owner for M1 in the ownership fields required by FAR 52.204-17. FPDS and USAspending also do not show a corporate parent on award records. In plain terms, there is no public parent company on file.

You can confirm this in a few minutes in SAM.gov and USAspending. I outline the exact clicks below. Note that beneficial ownership filed under the Corporate Transparency Act is not public, so you will not find individual ultimate owners online. What you can see, and what matters for teaming and risk, is whether the company reports an immediate owner or a highest-level owner in SAM, and whether FPDS ties the vendor to a parent DUNS or CAGE.

## Is M1 Support Services publicly traded or owned by a PE-backed rollup?

No. There is no SEC filer profile for M1 Support Services, and no corporate parent is reported in SAM.gov or on FPDS awards. If a private equity sponsor or strategic buyer had acquired M1 and consolidated reporting, SAM would typically show a highest-level owner name and CAGE in the Ownership Details section required by FAR 52.204-17. That field is not populated for M1.

If you see blogs guessing otherwise, check the data yourself using the steps below.

## How to verify M1 ownership in SAM.gov, step by step

You do not need a paid tool. You need a free SAM.gov account to see full entity details.

1. Go to SAM.gov, select Search, then Entities. In the keyword box, type M1 Support Services and filter by Active registration.
2. Open the Entity Registration record for M1 Support Services, L.P. Confirm the UEI and CAGE are present. If you need a refresher on what these are, see our glossaries for the [UEI](/glossary/uei/) and [CAGE code](/glossary/cage-code/).
3. In the left menu, open Representations and Certifications, then FAR 52.204-17 Ownership or Control of Offeror. This clause requires disclosure of Immediate Owner and Highest-Level Owner names and CAGE codes, if applicable.
4. Look at these specific fields inside 52.204-17:
 - Immediate Owner Name and CAGE.
 - Highest-Level Owner Name and CAGE.
 - Ownership type, such as company or individual.
5. For M1 Support Services, L.P., you will find that Highest-Level Owner is not populated. Immediate Owner, if shown, will be its general partner or a closely related LLC. No external parent is listed. Take a screenshot for your capture file.

Why this matters: FAR 4.18 and 52.204-17 make these disclosures part of the official record that contracting officers see. If a parent exists and is reportable, it should be here.

## Cross-check in FPDS and USAspending for parent indicators

Federal award systems capture parent data from the old DUNS hierarchy and current SAM feeds. It is not perfect, but it is a useful second check.

Do this:

1. Open USAspending.gov. Search for Awarding Agency equals Air Force or Army, Recipient Name contains M1 Support Services. Filter to Contract Awards.
2. Open any recent definitive contract or IDV task order. In the Award Summary, look for Recipient and Parent Recipient details. If FPDS has a parent, you will see a Parent Recipient Name or a Parent DUNS on legacy records.
3. For M1 Support Services, awards do not show a separate Parent Recipient. They list M1 as the recipient, with no parent recorded. Capture that as a second screenshot.

## State filings, executive names, and why BOI is not public

If you need the legal structure, check the Texas Secretary of State for M1 Support Services, L.P. You will see the registered agent and the general partner entity on the formation or registration documents. Expect the general partner to be an LLC with a similar name. That LLC often appears as the Immediate Owner in SAM.

A few important points:

- Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership reports filed with FinCEN started in 2024. They are not public. You cannot pull UBO names from a government website.
- FAR 52.204-10 covers executive compensation reporting. If the two FFATA thresholds are met, the contractor reports top five executives. Those are officers, not necessarily owners.
- Paid business credit files may list a UBO. Treat that as a lead, not as fact, unless you can backstop it with legal filings or a direct statement from the company.

## Why ownership status matters for capture and teaming

Ownership affects four things you will feel in a live pursuit:

- Affiliation and size status. Under 13 CFR 121.103, affiliation can combine receipts or employees across entities under common control. If you are pursuing a small business set-aside, a hidden parent can kill eligibility. M1 is not postured as a small business, but you still need to apply this logic to any teammate.
- Organizational conflicts. FAR 9.5 issues get worse inside corporate families. Parent or sister entities holding systems engineering or services work can create unequal access or impaired objectivity conflicts.
- Novation risk. FAR 42.1204 governs novation. If a parent acquisition is brewing, you can face disruption during your period of performance or proposal. Ask pointed questions before you sign a CTA or subcontract.
- Facility clearance continuity. For classified work, ownership changes can trigger a FOCI review under NISPOM. That means delays that can affect staffing and readiness.

## Common pitfalls when people research ownership

- Confusing executives with owners. FAR 52.204-10 executive compensation is not ownership.
- Assuming DUNS family links equal current ownership. DUNS hierarchies trail reality and are not the same as the FAR 52.204-17 owner fields.
- Treating LinkedIn as definitive. Job titles do not convey equity or control.
- Missing novation notices. When ownership does change, the public breadcrumb is often a Contracting Officer memorandum in a modification record citing FAR 42.1204. Search for mods with authority code N, novation agreement attached.
