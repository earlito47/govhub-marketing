## Start here: Section L tells you what to submit, Section M tells you how it will be scored

Read Section L once for tasks and format, then Section M for scoring, then map them line by line. If L says “submit a 20 page Technical Volume with a Staffing Plan” and M scores “Technical Approach” and “Staffing,” you have two required sections in your outline. If M does not mention something L asks for, you still include it for compliance, but you do not waste pages on it. You spend pages on what M will score.

Confirm the source clauses. For commercial items you will see FAR 52.212-1 and 52.212-2. For negotiated procurements you will see FAR 52.215-1 and Part 15 references. Identify LPTA under FAR 15.101-2 or Tradeoff under FAR 15.101-1. Then build a quick compliance matrix so nothing slips.

## Where L and M live in the FAR

Section L and Section M are part of the Uniform Contract Format in FAR 15.204-1. Section L is “Instructions, conditions, and notices to offerors.” Section M is “Evaluation factors for award.”

You will still see L and M on many FAR Part 12 commercial RFPs, often embedded as addenda to 52.212-1 and 52.212-2. For RFQs under Part 13 or task orders under IDIQs, the structure can be looser, but the same logic applies. Find the instructions. Find the evaluation. Map them.

## Map Section L to Section M in 15 minutes

- Skim Section M first. List the factors and subfactors in order. Note weight statements like “Technical is significantly more important than Price,” or “All non-price factors, when combined, are approximately equal to Price,” which comes from FAR 15.304(d).
- Read Section L once, highlighting anything that starts with “Provide,” “Include,” or “The Offeror shall.”
- Note every hard rule. Page limits, font, margins, file names, portal, and due date and time.
- Build a two-column list. Column 1 is each L requirement. Column 2 is the M factor it supports. If it does not support any factor, tag it “compliance only.”
- Draft your volume outline from that list. Headings must match M factors. Content under each heading must deliver the L items that support that factor.
- Flag gaps and conflicts for Q&A.

## Decode evaluation method fast

- LPTA, FAR 15.101-2. Non-price factors are pass or fail. If you pass, lowest price wins. Do not over-engineer the technical writeup. Focus on clear compliance and price.
- Best value tradeoff, FAR 15.101-1. The Government may pay more for higher merit. Look for adjectives like “more important than Price,” and for detailed rating schemes. Invest pages where M puts weight.
- Commercial items, 52.212-2. Often lists factors like Technical, Past Performance, and Price, sometimes with relative order but thin detail. Ask clarifying questions if “recent” or “relevant” are not defined.

## Page limits, format rules, and instant rejection risks

Section L sets traps. Miss them and you can be eliminated without a read.

- Fonts and margins. If L says 12 point Times New Roman and 1 inch margins, do that. No exceptions. Do not use condensed fonts unless L allows it.
- Page limits. Clarify what counts. Covers, tabs, resumes, appendices, and past performance questionnaires may or may not count. If L is silent, assume they count.
- File types and names. If L requires separate PDFs named “Vol1_Technical_OfferorName,” follow it exactly.
- Submittal portal and time zone. 52.215-1(c)(3) late proposal rule is strict. For commercial, see 52.212-1(f). Submit early and verify upload.

## Build a quick compliance matrix

A compliance matrix is the fastest way to control L and M. If you do not already have one, start simple.

- Columns to include: RFP reference, exact instruction text, owner, due date, volume, section, response status, and linked M factor.
- Add notes for pass or fail items like active SAM registration under FAR 52.204-7, required representations 52.204-8, and small business set-aside eligibility.
- Track every submission artifact. Forms, resumes, price files, and any separate past performance questionnaires.

## Worked example: shred L and M and outline your volumes

Scenario. The RFP cites FAR 52.215-1 and provides Sections L and M. Section L says:

- Volume I, Technical, 20 pages, 12 point Times New Roman, include a Management Approach, Technical Approach, and Staffing Plan.
- Volume II, Past Performance, 10 pages, include up to three recent and relevant contracts, with CPARS and points of contact.
- Volume III, Price, separate PDF using the provided spreadsheet. No page limit.

Section M says:

- Factor 1, Technical Approach, more important than Factor 2.
- Factor 2, Past Performance, approximately equal to Price.
- Factor 3, Price.
- “Technical is significantly more important than the combined weight of Past Performance and Price.” Adjectival ratings will be used per FAR 15.305.

Your mapping and outline:

- Technical Volume outline includes headings that mirror M: Technical Approach, then a subsection for Management and a subsection for Staffing that feed Technical merit. You spend most pages on Technical Approach because M weights it highest.
- Past Performance Volume includes three projects. Define “recent” and “relevant” by reading M or the PPQ. If not defined, assume 5 years for services and similar scope, magnitude, and complexity. State it in your writeup and ask a question if time allows.
- Price Volume follows the provided spreadsheet. If M notes price realism, explain basis of estimate. If not, avoid unsolicited narrative.

Checklist you can copy:

- Copy M factors as Level 1 headings in your outline.
- List every shall and must from L under the heading it supports.
- Mark each list item as compliance only or scored.
- Allocate pages by M importance. Heavier weight gets more pages.
- Insert cross-references in footers or sidebars that cite L paragraph and M factor at each section.
- Validate format and page counts against L one more time before Red Team.

## Handle inconsistencies and missing definitions

Conflicts between L and M happen. Here is how to deal with them.

- Missing definition of recent or relevant. Ask during Q&A. If time is gone, define your assumption in your proposal and state why it meets the Government’s interest.
- L lists a section that M does not score. Include it, but keep it tight.
- M cites a factor that L does not ask you to submit. Add the content and call out where it lives. The Government can only score what you submit.
- Inconsistent page counts across L subsections. Follow the most restrictive rule and raise it in Q&A.

## Commercial item versus negotiated RFPs, clause clues

If you see 52.212-1 and 52.212-2, expect lighter narrative and more emphasis on conformance. M may be brief. Still map everything and write to the factors.

If you see 52.215-1, expect formal L and M, tradeoff language, and detailed instructions. FAR 15.304 controls factors and their relative importance. Look for statements like “all evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are significantly more important than cost or price.”

## Past performance and experience, how it is scored

Past performance is usually its own factor, or it sits under Technical. FAR 15.305(a)(2) allows use of CPARS and questionnaires. Section L often caps the number of projects and defines recent and relevant.

Do the hard part. If your best projects are older than the window, say so and explain why they are still probative. If you have no CPARS, provide points of contact who will answer. If M allows subcontractor past performance, use it and label roles clearly.

## Oral presentations, samples, and down selects

Section L may require orals, product samples, or a two phase down select. Treat those as separate volumes in your matrix. Look in M for how they will be scored. If orals are not scored in M, they are usually pass or fail. Ask if unclear.

## Submission timing and portals, the late rule

The late rule is not a suggestion. 52.215-1(c)(3)(ii)(A) says a late offer will not be considered unless it was received at the Government installation and was under Government control before the deadline, or if there is an emergency that prevents timely receipt. 52.212-1(f) says similar for commercial items.

If L requires submission in PIEE, Symphony, or another portal, budget time for account setup. Upload early and confirm file integrity. If email is allowed, follow size limits and naming exactly.

## Turn L and M into reviews that catch issues early

Set up Pink and Red Team reviews against M factors, not your favorite outline. Reviewers score each section using the same factors and adjectives the Government will. That keeps comments focused.

If you want a quick primer on review stages and what to expect at each gate, read this: [Pink, Red, and Gold Team Reviews Explained](/blog/color-team-reviews-pink-red-gold/).

## Common traps that kill otherwise good proposals

- Ignoring LPTA signals and writing an essay no one will score.
- Missing a file, a signature, or a fillable form cited in L.
- Burying the factor language. If M says “demonstrates understanding,” use those words in your headings.
- Failing to map cross references. If the Government uses a checklist, make it easy for them to check your boxes.

## Quick reference: thresholds and forms that often show up

- Simplified Acquisition Threshold, 250,000 dollars. Under FAR Part 13, structure can be lighter.
- Micro-purchase threshold, 10,000 dollars. Often purchased with a card, no L and M.
- SF 33, Solicitation, Offer and Award. Common for Part 15 RFPs. Check Blocks 9, 11, and 12 for due dates, FOB, and delivery info.
- Representations and certifications. Verify in SAM and complete any addenda. See 52.204-7 and 52.204-8.
- Subcontracting plan. If you are other than small and the value exceeds 750,000 dollars for supplies and services, see 52.219-9. For construction, 1.5 million dollars.

Read L to comply. Write to M to win. Do both with a matrix and you avoid most unforced errors.
